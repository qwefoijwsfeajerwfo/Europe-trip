Climate & Best Time to Travel
Barcelona
Best: Apr–Jun / Sep–Oct
Mediterranean climate — hot and dry in summer (30°C+), mild in spring and autumn (18–25°C). Spring avoids the worst heat and the massive July/August tourist crowds. Outdoor sightseeing is comfortable all day.
Rome
Best: Apr–May / Sep–Oct
Also Mediterranean but more humid in summer. Spring hits the sweet spot — clear skies, 20–26°C, and far fewer tourists than peak season. Avoid July and August — hot, crowded and expensive.
Interlaken
Best: Jun–Sep
Alpine climate — snow in winter, warm and lush in summer. June to September gives clear mountain views, ideal hiking conditions and daytime temps around 20–25°C. Winter is for skiing, not sightseeing.
Amsterdam
Best: Apr–May / Sep
Temperate maritime — mild but unpredictable. April to May is tulip season with pleasant temps around 15–18°C. Pack a rain jacket no matter when you go. Summers are warm (~20°C) but can still be rainy.

Step 3

Key Considerations

🛂
Official Documents
A valid Australian passport is required to enter all four countries. Your passport needs at least 6 months validity left after your return date — renew it early as Australian passports take around 4 to 6 weeks to process.

Spain, Italy, Switzerland and the Netherlands are all Schengen Area countries, meaning Australian citizens can enter without a visa for up to 90 days. However, from late 2025 the EU is introducing ETIAS — a travel authorisation that costs around €7 and must be applied for online before travelling. It is not a visa but it is required.

Always carry both physical and digital copies of your passport and all booking confirmations, stored separately from the originals.
🛡️
Travel Insurance
Travel insurance should be purchased as soon as flights are booked, not right before departure. If something goes wrong beforehand — a medical emergency or cancelled flight — you are only covered from the date the policy was purchased.

The policy needs to cover medical emergencies, trip cancellation or interruption, lost or stolen luggage and flight delays. Healthcare in Europe is expensive without insurance, especially in Switzerland.

Australian providers like Cover-More, 1Cover and Allianz offer comprehensive international policies. For 20 days in Europe expect to pay around $150–$250 AUD per person. Register your trip at smartraveller.gov.au.
🌍
Language & Culture
Each destination has different languages and cultural expectations worth knowing before you arrive.

Barcelona: Spanish and Catalan spoken. Learning "gracias" and "por favor" goes a long way. English is widely spoken in tourist areas.

Rome: Dress codes matter — cover shoulders and knees to enter the Vatican and most churches or you won't be allowed in.

Interlaken: German-speaking region. Punctuality is serious — trains leave exactly on time.

Amsterdam: Nearly everyone speaks fluent English. Respect bike lanes — walking in them genuinely frustrates locals and can be dangerous.
Financial Trouble Overseas
Common Problems
Card stolen or skimmed at an ATM, unexpected overseas transaction fees, dynamic currency conversion scams where a merchant charges you in AUD at a much worse rate, running out of money from underestimating costs — Switzerland is significantly more expensive than the other cities. Medical emergencies without insurance can also create very large unexpected bills.
What To Do
Contact your bank immediately to freeze stolen cards through their app. Always carry a backup card stored separately — a Wise card is a good option with low foreign transaction fees. Keep emergency cash in your hotel safe. Tell your bank your travel dates before you leave, otherwise overseas spending can trigger an automatic account freeze.
Who To Contact
The Australian Embassy or Consulate is the main contact. Spain: +34 91 353 6600. Italy: +39 06 852721. Netherlands: +31 70 310 8200. They assist with lost passports, medical situations and can help organise emergency funds through family.

Smartraveller 24/7 line: +61 2 6261 3305 — can be called from overseas any time.
